A private loan may involve relatives, friends, partners or acquaintances. Trust makes clarity more important, not less: document the amount, rate, payment dates and consequences of late or early payment.

This article covers the arithmetic, not legal or tax advice. Rules vary by jurisdiction; seek professional help for a large amount, collateral, material interest or meaningful risk of dispute.

## Terms to document

- Principal and disbursement date.
- Whether interest is charged and at what rate.
- Term, payment frequency and due dates.
- Treatment of late and early payments.
- Evidence required for each payment.
- Parties’ identities and signatures where appropriate.

## Equal-payment calculation

For an interest-bearing fixed-payment loan:

```txt
payment = P x [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]
```

For a zero-interest agreement:

```txt
payment = principal / number of payments
```

For `$20,000` at 8% a year over 12 months, the estimated payment is `$1,739.77`, total interest is about `$877.22` and total repayment is `$20,877.22`.

## Legal and tax considerations

A calculation does not replace a properly drafted agreement. Retain transfer records and define interest, default and prepayment in writing. Consult a qualified local adviser about promissory notes, collateral and tax reporting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can individuals charge one another interest?

That depends on applicable law and the agreement. Obtain local legal and tax advice before doing so.

### Is an amortization schedule worthwhile?

Yes. It reduces ambiguity about the balance, interest and payment history.
