The smallest monthly payment is not necessarily the cheapest loan. It may reflect a longer term and therefore more interest, or omit fees and insurance.

## Put both offers on the same basis

Collect the amount requested, net cash received, annual rate, official CAT, term, payment frequency, all-in payment, origination fee, insurance and prepayment rules. Compare the same principal and term whenever possible.

For example, at 24% over 36 months with no fee or insurance:

| Principal | Base payment | Estimated interest | Total payments |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $50,000 | $1,961.64 | $20,619.14 | $70,619.14 |
| $100,000 | $3,923.29 | $41,238.27 | $141,238.27 |

At the same rate, repaying `$50,000` over 24 months raises the payment to `$2,643.55` but lowers interest to `$13,445.32`. Affordability and cost move in opposite directions.

## Compare CAT and total repayment

Mexico’s CAT aims to annualize the broader cost of credit under defined assumptions; total repayment tells you how much cash leaves your pocket. Use both. A lower interest rate can still lose once fees, insurance and a longer term are included.

## Worked comparison

| Item | Loan A | Loan B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Annual rate | 24% | 18% |
| Term | 24 months | 36 months |
| Origination fee | $0 | $1,000 |
| Monthly insurance | $0 | $150 |
| All-in monthly payment | $2,643.55 | $1,957.62 |
| Estimated interest | $13,445.32 | $15,074.31 |
| Total insurance | $0 | $5,400 |
| Total cost | $63,445.32 | $71,474.31 |
| Approx. CAT | 26.82% | 28.53% |

Loan B advertises a lower rate and has a smaller monthly payment, yet costs more because it lasts longer and adds both a fee and insurance.

## Before signing

- Verify the amount you will actually receive.
- Confirm the all-in payment and total repayment.
- Review interest, fees, insurance and late charges.
- Check the official CAT and its assumptions.
- Ask whether early payments go to principal and whether a penalty applies.

Reproduce the offers in the [loan calculator](/en/finance/loan-calculator). For the Mexican disclosure metric, see [CAT vs. interest rate](/en/blog/finance/cat-vs-interest-rate); for upfront costs, see [loan origination fees](/en/blog/finance/loan-origination-fee).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a lower rate or lower CAT better?

CAT is broader, but neither figure replaces total repayment, net proceeds and contract terms.

### Should I choose the lowest monthly payment?

Not automatically. A longer term can reduce the payment while increasing interest.

### How do I compare different terms?

Compare total cost, CAT and duration, then model the same term to isolate rate and fee differences.
