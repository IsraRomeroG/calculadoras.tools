## Calculate the discount amount

Multiply the original price by the percentage written as a decimal:

```txt
discount amount = original price x discount / 100
```

For a `$1,200` item discounted by 25%:

```txt
1200 x 25 / 100 = 300
```

The discount is `$300`. See [how to calculate percentages](/en/blog/math/how-to-calculate-percentages) if you want to review the underlying operation.

## Calculate the final price

Subtract the discount from the original price:

```txt
1200 - 300 = 900
```

The sale price is `$900`. Equivalently, multiply the original price by the percentage that remains: `1200 x 0.75 = 900`.

## Be careful with stacked discounts

Successive discounts are not added directly. On a `$1,000` item, 20% off followed by an additional 10% gives:

```txt
1000 x 0.80 = 800
800 x 0.90 = 720
```

The effective discount is 28%, not 30%, because the second discount applies to the already reduced price. The full method is explained in [successive discounts](/en/blog/finance/successive-discounts).

## Compare the final amount payable

The headline percentage is only one part of a purchase. Include shipping, taxes, financing charges and eligibility restrictions before comparing offers. Use the [percentage calculator](/en/math/percentage-calculator) to check both the discount and final price.
