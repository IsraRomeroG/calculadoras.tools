## The difference in one minute

A nominal rate is a stated rate before the full effect of intra-year compounding is expressed as one annual return. It should therefore be paired with a frequency, such as “12% nominal annual rate, compounded monthly.”

An effective annual rate shows the one-year result after compounding. It is useful when products capitalize interest at different intervals.

In short, the nominal rate is the quoted reference rate; the effective rate reflects the annual effect of applying it.

## Effective annual rate formula

```txt
effective annual rate = (1 + r / n) ^ n - 1
```

`r` is the nominal annual rate as a decimal and `n` is the number of compounding periods per year.

For a 12% nominal annual rate compounded monthly:

```txt
monthly rate = 0.12 / 12 = 0.01
effective annual rate = (1 + 0.12 / 12) ^ 12 - 1
effective annual rate = 0.1268 = 12.68%
```

On `$10,000`, 12% compounded once a year produces `$11,200`. Monthly compounding produces approximately `$11,268.25`. The difference comes from reinvesting interest during the year.

## Why the distinction matters

A 12% nominal rate compounded monthly is not equivalent to a 12% effective annual rate: the former is about 12.68% effective. The distinction matters for deposits, investments and loans.

It is only one part of a product comparison. Fees, taxes, insurance and other charges may not be included. Acronyms such as APY, EAR, AER, TAE, TEA and Mexico’s CAT also have market-specific definitions; do not assume that similarly named figures include the same costs.

| Compounding | Periods per year | Effective rate from 12% nominal |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Annual | 1 | 12.00% |
| Monthly | 12 | 12.68% |
| Daily | 365 | 12.75% |

## Common mistakes

- Comparing a monthly rate directly with an annual rate.
- Applying compounding twice to a rate that is already effective.
- Ignoring fees and taxes when comparing net outcomes.
- Treating a converted rate as a guaranteed return.
- Assuming regulatory rate labels mean the same thing in every country.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which rate should I enter in a calculator?

If you have a nominal annual rate and its compounding frequency, enter both. If you already have an effective annual rate, use an annual basis so you do not count compounding twice.

### Does an effective rate include taxes or fees?

Not necessarily. The effective rate discussed here measures compounding only. Check the legal definition and disclosures for the product you are evaluating.

### Are APY, EAR, TAE and TEA interchangeable?

They may all aim to express an annualized figure, but their regulatory meaning and included costs vary by jurisdiction. Verify the official methodology.

## Before comparing rates

Convert every option to the same time basis and use comparable assumptions for fees and taxes. If you are measuring growth between a beginning and ending value over several years, the related metric is [CAGR](/en/blog/finance/cagr-compound-annual-growth-rate).
