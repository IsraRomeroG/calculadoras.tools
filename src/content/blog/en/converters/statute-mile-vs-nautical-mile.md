## The quick difference

A **statute mile** and a **nautical mile** are different units of distance. They cannot be interchanged using the same factor:

| Unit | Exact equivalent | Main use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Statute mile (`mi`) | 1.609344 km | Distances on land |
| Nautical mile (`nmi`) | 1.852 km | Maritime and air navigation |

The nautical mile is longer by `0.242656 km`, or about `243 meters`. One nautical mile is approximately `1.15078 mi`.

> **Important:** the [kilometers-to-miles converter](/en/converters/kilometers-to-miles) works with statute miles (`mi`). It does not accept nautical miles (`nmi`).

When a road sign, race or land distance simply says “miles,” it normally means statute miles. On nautical charts, sea routes and in air navigation, the context may indicate nautical miles.

## What a statute mile is

The statute mile, also called the international mile, is the common unit for road distances in countries such as the United States and the United Kingdom.

Its exact equivalent is:

```txt
1 mi = 1.609344 km
```

For example, a distance of `10 mi` is:

```txt
10 × 1.609344 = 16.09344 km
```

This article uses the symbol `mi`. Conventions can differ between publications, but writing the unit explicitly prevents confusion with a nautical mile.

The guide to [converting kilometers to miles](/en/blog/converters/how-to-convert-kilometers-to-miles) explains the formulas and rounding used for statute miles.

## What a nautical mile is

A nautical mile is a unit of distance used in maritime and air navigation. Its exact international equivalent is:

```txt
1 nmi = 1.852 km = 1852 m
```

[NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/nautical-mile-knot.html) explains that the unit relates to latitude and longitude: one nautical mile corresponds to one minute of latitude. This relationship makes it practical for charts and geographic positions.

This article uses `nmi` to distinguish it clearly. Charts, instruments and regulations may use other conventions, such as `NM`, so always check the legend or system documentation.

Do not enter a nautical-mile value into a calculator configured for statute miles. If you entered `10 nmi` as though it were `10 mi`, you would obtain `16.09344 km` instead of the correct `18.52 km`.

## Conversion table

The table applies the exact factors separately:

| Quantity | Statute miles to kilometers | Nautical miles to kilometers |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 mi = 1.609344 km | 1 nmi = 1.852 km |
| 5 | 5 mi = 8.04672 km | 5 nmi = 9.26 km |
| 10 | 10 mi = 16.09344 km | 10 nmi = 18.52 km |
| 50 | 50 mi = 80.4672 km | 50 nmi = 92.6 km |
| 100 | 100 mi = 160.9344 km | 100 nmi = 185.2 km |

For any other value:

```txt
kilometers = statute miles × 1.609344
kilometers = nautical miles × 1.852
```

The reverse operation also depends on the source unit:

```txt
statute miles = kilometers / 1.609344
nautical miles = kilometers / 1.852
```

As a reference, `100 km` is approximately `62.1371 mi` or `53.9957 nmi`. Both results describe the same distance in different units.

## A nautical mile and a knot are not the same

A nautical mile measures **distance**. A knot measures **speed**:

```txt
1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour
```

A vessel traveling at `10 knots` covers `10 nmi` in one hour if it maintains a constant speed. In kilometers per hour:

```txt
10 × 1.852 = 18.52 km/h
```

It is incorrect to say that a route “measures 10 knots,” because a knot does not measure length. Likewise, `10 nmi` alone does not describe speed because no time is specified.

## How to choose the correct factor

Identify the context and symbol before converting:

- For roads, cars, athletics and everyday land distances in customary-unit contexts, use the statute mile (`mi`).
- For sea routes, nautical charts and air navigation, check whether the distance is in nautical miles (`nmi`).
- A value in knots is a speed, not a distance.
- If a document only says “mile,” check its legend, source or context before choosing a factor.

Using `1.609344` instead of `1.852` creates a difference of about `13.1%` from the actual nautical distance. The error grows with the quantity: at `100` units, the two results differ by `24.2656 km`.

For more context on how these units relate to the metric system, read [kilometers vs. miles](/en/blog/converters/kilometers-vs-miles).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many kilometers are in a nautical mile?

One international nautical mile equals exactly `1.852 km`, or `1852 m`.

### Is a nautical mile longer than a statute mile?

Yes. A nautical mile is `1.852 km` and a statute mile is `1.609344 km`. The difference is `0.242656 km`, or about `243 m`.

### What is the difference between a nautical mile and a knot?

A nautical mile (`nmi`) is a unit of distance. A knot is a unit of speed equal to one nautical mile per hour.

### Does the kilometers-to-miles calculator convert nmi?

No. It converts kilometers and statute miles (`mi`). To convert nautical miles to kilometers, multiply the `nmi` value by `1.852`.

### Which mile is used on roads?

In road contexts, “mile” normally means a statute mile (`mi`). Nautical miles are reserved for maritime and air-navigation contexts.
