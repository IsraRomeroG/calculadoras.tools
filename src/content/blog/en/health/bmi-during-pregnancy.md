An adult BMI calculated from current pregnancy weight should not be interpreted in the usual way. Weight gain during pregnancy includes the fetus, placenta, amniotic fluid, increased blood volume and other normal physiological changes.

Clinical recommendations instead begin with **pre-pregnancy BMI**. For a singleton pregnancy, CDC guidance lists total weight-gain ranges of `28–40 lb` for pre-pregnancy underweight, `25–35 lb` for healthy weight, `15–25 lb` for overweight and `11–20 lb` for obesity. Recommendations differ for twins and other circumstances.

The [BMI calculator](/en/health/bmi-calculator) may help calculate a pre-pregnancy reference if you know your earlier weight, but it cannot assess whether your current gain is appropriate. Timing, fetal growth, symptoms and your medical history all matter.

Discuss a personalized target with your prenatal care team, especially with multiple pregnancy, severe nausea or vomiting, diabetes, hypertension or weight change outside the expected pattern. Do not use a general calorie deficit or adult BMI target during pregnancy without clinical guidance.
