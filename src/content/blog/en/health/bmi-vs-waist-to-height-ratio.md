BMI describes weight relative to height:

```txt
BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)^2
```

Waist-to-height ratio focuses on abdominal size:

```txt
Waist-to-height ratio = waist circumference / height
```

Use the same units for waist and height. A `82 cm` waist and `170 cm` height produce `0.48`.

The measures answer different questions. BMI is widely standardized and useful for population screening, but it cannot distinguish muscle from fat or show where fat is stored. Waist-to-height ratio adds information about central fat distribution, though measurement technique and cutoffs also introduce uncertainty.

Measure the waist consistently with a flexible tape, without compressing the skin, after a normal exhalation. Follow the exact anatomical site required by the guideline you are using because protocols differ.

Neither measure diagnoses disease. Review the [BMI calculator](/en/health/bmi-calculator), waist trend, blood pressure, laboratory results and family history together. A clinician can interpret conflicting results or elevated cardiometabolic risk.
