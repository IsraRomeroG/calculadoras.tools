BMI and daily calorie estimates answer different questions. BMI compares weight with height; TDEE estimates how much energy you use in a typical day.

```txt
BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)^2
TDEE = resting energy estimate x activity factor
```

BMI places most adults in a reference category, but it does not measure metabolism or prescribe an intake. TDEE uses age, sex, height, weight and activity to estimate maintenance calories, but it does not determine whether a weight is healthy.

Used together, the measures can provide context. Someone considering a change in weight might first review BMI as one screening indicator, then estimate TDEE to plan a modest energy adjustment. The plan should still account for muscle mass, waist measurement, medical history, food quality, sleep and training.

Both calculations have limitations. BMI can misclassify muscular people and requires different interpretation for children and pregnancy. TDEE depends heavily on an estimated activity level and should be refined against two or three weeks of real-world data.
