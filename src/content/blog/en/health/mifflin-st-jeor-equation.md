The Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates resting energy expenditure from weight, height, age and sex. In common calculator terminology, the result is often labelled BMR.

With weight in kilograms, height in centimetres and age in years:

```txt
Men:    10W + 6.25H - 5A + 5
Women:  10W + 6.25H - 5A - 161
```

For a 30-year-old woman weighing `65 kg` and measuring `165 cm`:

```txt
10(65) + 6.25(165) - 5(30) - 161
= 1,370 kcal/day approximately
```

That is a resting estimate, not maintenance calories. To approximate total daily energy expenditure, multiply it by an activity factor:

```txt
TDEE = resting estimate x activity factor
```

An activity factor of `1.55` would produce about `2,124 kcal/day`. The factor is often the largest source of uncertainty.

The original equation was developed from measured resting expenditure in 498 adults. Like every population equation, it can differ from an individual's measured metabolism, particularly when body composition or health differs from the study population.
