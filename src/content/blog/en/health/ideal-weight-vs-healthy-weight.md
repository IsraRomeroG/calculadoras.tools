“Ideal weight” often suggests that one exact number is right for every person of a given height. In reality, common ideal-weight equations were designed as rough references and do not capture body composition, age, ethnicity, health or personal history.

A healthy-weight **range** is more honest about that variation. For most adults, one common reference is the weight interval corresponding to BMI `18.5–24.9`:

```txt
Weight = BMI x height (m)^2
```

At `1.65 m`, that interval is approximately `50.4–67.8 kg`. It is a broad screening range, not a promise that every point is suitable or that values outside it are automatically unhealthy.

Muscle mass, waist measurement, blood pressure, laboratory markers, fitness and a sustainable lifestyle may be more informative than chasing a formula's central number. A stable weight that supports health and function is often a better goal than an arbitrary “ideal.”
