BMR and TDEE represent different levels of energy use.

**Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** is the energy required for essential functions under strict resting conditions. Online calculators usually estimate it from age, sex, height and weight rather than measure it directly.

**Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE)** adds the energy associated with digestion, everyday movement and exercise. A common approximation is:

```txt
TDEE = estimated BMR x activity factor
```

If estimated BMR is `1,500 kcal/day` and the activity factor is `1.55`, estimated TDEE is `2,325 kcal/day`. That larger number is normally the more relevant starting point for maintenance calories.

BMR is not a recommended minimum intake or a personalized eating target. A person's nutritional needs and safe intake cannot be decided from that figure alone. TDEE is also imperfect because activity factors simplify a variable week.

Use the [TDEE calculator](/en/health/tdee-calculator) to see both estimates. Compare the TDEE result with two or three weeks of real-world trend data, and revise it when body weight or routine changes substantially.
