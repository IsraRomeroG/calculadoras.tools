For most adults, a BMI from `18.5` through `24.9` is classified as the healthy-weight range. Below `18.5` is underweight, `25.0–29.9` is overweight and `30.0` or above is obesity.

There is no universally “best” number inside the range. A BMI of `20`, `22` or `24` can all fall within it, and the most appropriate weight depends on the individual's build, muscle, weight history and health.

At boundaries, calculate before rounding. An exact value of `24.96` is below `25`, even though a whole-number display could show `25`. A calculator that preserves decimals prevents this ambiguity.

A healthy-range BMI does not prove that body composition, blood pressure, blood glucose or fitness is healthy. Likewise, a value just outside the range does not diagnose a disease. BMI is best treated as one screening measure alongside waist size, clinical markers and personal context.

Use the [BMI calculator](/en/health/bmi-calculator) for your exact result. These adult cutoffs do not apply to children or teenagers, and pregnancy requires different interpretation.
