Begin by estimating your maintenance calories, or TDEE. A calorie deficit means consuming less energy than you expend on average.

```txt
Target intake = estimated TDEE - calorie deficit
```

If maintenance is approximately `2,200 kcal/day`, a modest `10%` reduction produces about `1,980 kcal/day`; a `15%` reduction gives about `1,870 kcal/day`. Percentages are only planning examples—not universal prescriptions.

A larger deficit is not automatically better. Excessive restriction can make it difficult to meet nutrient needs and may worsen hunger, fatigue, training performance and adherence. Favor a sustainable plan with adequate protein, fibre-rich foods and resistance training when appropriate.

Assess the trend over at least two or three weeks. Use weekly average weight because water, sodium, digestion and the menstrual cycle can obscure fat loss on individual days. If the trend is unchanged, first review tracking and activity assumptions, then make a small adjustment.

Use the [calorie deficit calculator](/en/health/calorie-deficit-calculator) to explore a starting range. Seek professional advice if you are pregnant, under 18, have a history of disordered eating, take weight-affecting medication or have a medical condition.
